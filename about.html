I didn’t go to art school, but I grew up in one. My mother is a professional fine artist, and I spent a lot of my childhood in her classes — watching her work, absorbing the language of drawing and composition and value before I had words for any of it. She’s been my painting coach these past four years as I’ve learned to translate what I absorbed into practice.

What finally brought me to the easel was the color of the water. We fish a lot — rivers, lakes, shallow water over colored rock — and I reached a point where I needed a way to hold onto what I was seeing. Painting turned out to be that.

I’m based in Montana, and most of what I paint is Glacier National Park and the wilderness around it. I take liberties with light, but I get the mountains right. Geographic accuracy matters to me. If you’ve stood at that lake, I want you to recognize it.

Four years in, I’m still finding out what I can do. I spent six months on Coastal Reckoning — a four-by-five-foot canvas my daughter gave me for Mother’s Day, because I’d once said out loud that I wondered what it would feel like to paint really large. I spent a year on a painting of my husband fishing, getting the gesture of his cast right, the way light hit the water. His family recognized him immediately — not from his face, but from how he stands.

— Marnie Henry
